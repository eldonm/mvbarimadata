1. The manifest and the complement

Of the 116 passengers on the manifest, Minister Edghill stated 100 booked through the Online Booking System and 16 through Mobile Money Guyana. The Associated Press reports that clerks had sold unmanifested seats for cash “for decades at state-run ferry terminals.”
Edghill stated on 19 July that 35 of the 67 then rescued appeared on the manifest, and that authorities could no longer determine how many had been aboard. DPI releases of 19 July state that families were being updated using the vessel’s manifest.

2. Stated capacity limits

Officials gave different limits on different days. Asked when the 1938 rating was raised, MARAD’s Director-General said: “I don’t have that information.”
Three of the four stated limits sit below the 268 tonnes the vessel was manifested to carry; the fourth, Edghill’s 284 tonnes, sits above it. Edghill’s own line-by-line audit on 19 July returned 260 tonnes. On 22 July MARAD stated the vessel was “not overloaded,” citing the load line. No published certificate appears in the corpus against which any of these figures can be checked.

3. Recovery and survivor figures

Bodies recovered reached 73 on 24 July and were unchanged through 26 July. Survivors were revised from 77 to 76 after de-duplication, per News Room and Kaieteur. Kaieteur published its own tally of 103 on 22 July, compiled from Coast Guard, opposition, private-vessel and fishermen’s accounts, on a day the government reported 53.

4. The voyage, hour by hour

15:15
Departs the T&HD wharf at Kingston, Georgetown, for Port Kaituma. 116 passengers and 17 crew on the manifest.
~19:00
Survivor Leon Murray (AP) states one engine failed about four hours out and the crew repaired it at sea rather than turning back.
~20:00
Murray states a passenger reported water on the lower deck and was told: “They told us this is normal.”
23:01
A Mayday is widely reported at this time. On 25 July the Prime Minister said of a Mayday from the captain: “We have no such record.”
01:36
Capt. Gouveia takes off from Ogle in a private fixed-wing Islander, 2h 35m after the reported Mayday, and reports coordinates from a sighted flare. Two military helicopters were reported unserviceable.
03:15
Edghill announces eight retrieved alive. Guyana Times reports fisherman Haresh Singh located the hull.
Survivor accounts describe a packed lower deck (Murray), cargo stacked at the stern (Elena Moonsammy) and a list that did not right itself (Wayne Kitson). MARAD’s Director-General stated that a vessel with positive stability returns upright even when heeled twenty degrees, and attributed the failure to right to operational error in treacherous conditions. The two accounts are not reconciled in any source. Reported sinking duration ranges from about four minutes (Murray) to “less than a minute” (Guyana Times).

1939
Built by Ferguson Brothers, Port Glasgow, ordered through the Crown Agents for the Colonies — one of three sisters for British Guiana Railways, per the Paxman engine register.
1970
MV Christena capsizes off St Kitts–Nevis: a reported 233 dead, more than 320 aboard against a certified 155. One source places her construction at the same Georgetown yard as Guyana’s ferries.
2010 · 2015
Port Kaituma residents call for better vessels. A Kaieteur inspection describes the Barima’s deck and winch area as “a mass of junk and rust”; Capt. R. E. W. Adams writes such a vessel would be scrapped elsewhere in the Caribbean.
2017 · 2019
G$150.6m spent rehabilitating the hull rather than replacing it. The sister ship is reported withdrawn, leaving the Barima the only vessel on the Port Kaituma run — though Kaieteur reported on 24 July that it “continues operating.”
2020 · 2023
A DPI release records mechanical failures on the vessel. The US$12.7m MV Ma Lisha is commissioned and does not run the route — for want of wharf works (PM Phillips) or cargo capacity (residents). Twenty children die in the Mahdia dormitory fire.
2025
A DPI release states the Transport & Harbours Department has “longstanding issues of negligence, mismanagement, and accountability.” Published sixteen months before the sinking.
2026
A further G$124.5m tendered for the hull in March, within a G$11.2bn river-transport allocation; no line for retiring the vessel appears in the corpus. Kaieteur reports a newer vessel uncommissioned at the wharf since May. Stabroek News ceases print in March.

7. Where the parties stand on the inquiry, as of 26 July 2026

The Government
President Ali named the five-member Commission of Inquiry on 26 July: Justice Godfrey Phillip Smith of Belize as chair, with Capt. Hamada Fouda (Jamaica), Nyree Dawn Alfonso (Trinidad and Tobago), Dr Andrzej Jasionowski (Poland) and Rear Admiral (Ret’d) Hayden Pritchard (Trinidad and Tobago). Terms of reference were announced covering loading, boarding, seaworthiness, maintenance history, crew competence, lifesaving arrangements, weather and the search-and-rescue response. The establishing instruments were described as still being finalised; no statutory basis, secretary or reporting deadline was cited. [SINGLE SOURCE]
The parliamentary opposition
APNU states the National Assembly should set the terms of reference and include IMO representatives, and has tabled a motion to empower a commission to summon witnesses and compel documents. WIN seeks the recusal of Ministers Edghill and Indar, livestreamed hearings and publication of the full report. The AFC proposes the Commission of Inquiry Act, Cap. 19:03. Jointly, five parties state a Commission of Inquiry is not a substitute for a marine casualty investigation.
Civil society
The GHRA rejects executive appointment and proposes a parliamentary commission with equal government and opposition membership, a judicially qualified chair preferably from elsewhere in the Caribbean, a statutory duty of candour and state-funded counsel for bereaved families. TIGI and Rescue Guyana call for an IMO-led investigation. The APA calls for Minister Edghill’s removal. The National Toshaos Council urges a “comprehensive, transparent, independent review.”
