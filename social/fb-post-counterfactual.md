Since the MV Barima Tragedy, I had AI collect every document published about it. First I asked: what doesn't add up? Now I've asked a harder question — if the Government had an AI advisor from the night the boat went down, whose only job was keeping the people's trust, what would it have told them to do?

Here's the answer, day by day.

Same method as before: Claude on Opus 5, reasoning over the whole archive — now 295 published documents across national and international media houses, state releases and social media, as of 1 August 2026. Everything grounded, no agenda.

But this one comes with a warning. NONE OF IT HAPPENED. It is an alternate timeline. What makes it worth reading is that almost none of it is the AI's own idea — nearly every step below was asked for in public at the time, by lawyers, village councils, opposition MPs, newspapers, a retired ship pilot. The advice was given. It just wasn't taken.

And it saves nobody. The ship still sinks at 11 that Saturday night. About 103 people still do not come home. What changes is only what the Government said and showed afterwards — which turns out to be nearly the whole difference between being trusted and being suspected.

1. SUN 19 JULY — Blame nobody on day one. No "mischief". Instead: the ship was licensed for 397 people and carried 250 life jackets, that does not add up, and I do not yet know why. Both numbers were public that day. Nobody set them side by side, and fourteen days on, nobody in government has explained them.

2. MON 20 JULY — Publish the rescue log, gaps and all. And say what the wharf video shows about the CARGO, not just how many people boarded. The state has had that video since day two. It told us the headcount. It has never said one word about the cargo — the thing three men were charged over.

3. TUE 21 JULY — One set of numbers, every day. On board, saved, found, named, still missing — and the total who did not come home. The Government publishes bodies found. It has never once published the total. Newspapers had to work it out by subtraction.

4. WED 22 JULY — MARAD steps back. It passed this ship as fit to sail, so it cannot be the body that decides whether the ship was fit. Hand the whole file to investigators who don't work for the owner.

5. THU 23 JULY — Two ministers step aside. Not resign — step aside, so they are not running the department the inquiry is examining. The Transparency Institute asked for exactly this, naming both of them, on exactly that day. No answer to it appears anywhere in the record.

6. THU 23 JULY — Ask about the deadline. On one published reading of the Transport and Harbours Act, families have six months to start a claim: expiring about 18 January 2027, with written notice due by about 18 December 2026. In fourteen days nobody in Government has said the word "limitation" once. This is the one item on the page that is not history. It is a clock, and it is running now.

7. SAT 25 JULY — Nothing goes to grieving families about the wreck until the divers have reported and the prosecutor has said in writing whether the ship is needed as evidence. In real life the memorial paper went to families on 25 July. The divers said the wreck was too dangerous to enter on the 27th. The reason arrived two days after the request.

8. TUE 28 JULY — Settle how the inquiry will take evidence BEFORE any charging decision is announced. Four ways of doing it were written out and published. The inquiry was sworn in the next day with no rules at all. Nobody chose one.

The page carries on past today — month by month to how this could end in 2027 — and it marks plainly where the record stops and the guesswork starts.

One line it will not cross: three men are charged and have not been tried. Nothing on that page says what they did, or what should happen to them.
