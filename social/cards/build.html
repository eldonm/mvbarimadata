Reason 1

There may be no papers to show

Guyana’s sea laws may not cover a boat this size at all. No rule on life jackets. No rule to count passengers. Maybe no rule requiring the certificate everyone keeps asking for.

If true: nobody hid the paper. It was never made.

Reason 2

They picked a story on day one

One day after she sank — before anyone investigated anything — the minister named the cause in one word: “mischief.” He put it on the workers at the wharf.

Nine days later, three workers were charged with murder. Nobody above them was charged with anything.

If true: they are protecting a story, not a secret.

Reason 3

Money. The state has to pay

The boat had no insurance. The government carries the cost itself.

Families got $350,000 for funerals — and the government said clearly this is not compensation. Real compensation waits on an inquiry with no end date.

If true: every month of delay costs the state less — and the families more.

Reason 4

A $1.4 billion contract

G$1.4bn was paid out in 2022 for a new wharf at Port Kaituma. Almost four years later it is still not finished.

So the newer ferry couldn’t go there. An 87-year-old boat kept the run — the Prime Minister has admitted this. The inquiry’s list does not name that contract.

If true: the thing that put an old boat on that route is the one thing not being looked at.

Reason 5

They may simply not know how

Guyana has never properly investigated a sea disaster. No trained team. No law setting out how it is done.

So the government did the only things it knew: send police, charge people, call an inquiry. It looks like hiding.

If true: not wicked. Just a country with nothing built to handle this.

Reason 6 — most likely

All of it at once

No single secret. A country whose laws asked for almost nothing. A government that chose a story too fast. And three expensive reasons never to change it: ministers’ jobs, a $1.4 billion contract, and a bill for a hundred lives.

You don’t need a conspiracy. You need people who guessed on day one, and found it cost more to admit it every day after.
