Guyanese press news 26 July 2026

No new development in search and recovery operations

Kaieteur News · Khadidja Ba

Read the original at Kaieteur News ↗

What this source establishes

The most recent article in this corpus, recording that recovery has stalled at 73 bodies. It documents the burial of brothers Omarius, 6, and Orwin Europe, 8, in a shared grave at Port Kaituma with their classmates present, and confirms more than thirty funerals held. Its central news is the reaction to the Commission of Inquiry: the Leader of the Opposition says neither he nor any parliamentary opposition party was consulted, and a joint WIN, APNU, FGM, AFC and VPAC statement demands a separate technical marine safety investigation under the IMO Casualty Investigation Code and SOLAS standards.

Figures cited in this source

73 bodies recovered, 69 identified, 63 released; 76 survivors; working manifest of 179 aboard; more than 30 funerals held

Key claims

  • Two young brothers, six-year-old Omarius and eight-year-old Orwin Europe, students of Port Kaituma Primary School, were buried in a shared grave in Port Kaituma.
  • Minister of Amerindian Affairs Sarah Browne said at the funeral: 'There is nothing that I can say at this point that can bring them back.'
  • A separate service in Port Kaituma honoured Maryann Bernard.
  • Construction crews in Mabaruma and White Water continue building tombs as bodies are returned to home communities.
  • More than thirty funerals have taken place across the region so far.
  • Authorities confirmed there would be no press briefing Saturday evening as efforts had yielded no new developments since Friday.
  • President Ali announced a Commission of Inquiry including experts in law, marine engineering, maritime safety and disaster investigation, drawing criticism over lack of prior consultation.
  • The Leader of the Opposition said neither he nor any parliamentary opposition party was consulted in establishing the Commission.
  • A joint opposition statement by WIN, APNU, FGM, AFC and VPAC welcomed the inquiry in principle but criticised its timing, noting it was announced shortly before Parliament was scheduled to debate opposition motions.
  • The opposition coalition demanded a separate technical marine safety investigation under the IMO's Casualty Investigation Code and SOLAS standards.
  • The coalition demanded the immediate resignation or leave of absence of Ministers Juan Edghill and Deodat Indar.
  • The MV Barima, built in 1938, was deployed on the route because the newer MV Ma Lisha cannot dock at Port Kaituma due to delayed wharf upgrades.
  • MARAD Director General Captain Stephen Thomas could not explain when or why the Barima's certified capacity was modified from its original 1938 rating of 120 tons of cargo and 150 passengers.
  • Some Venezuelan relatives publicly disputed the government's account, alleging family members were buried in earthen graves against their explicit requests.

Quotations

  • “There is nothing that I can say at this point that can bring them back. As a mother, I feel your pain... I don't know how a mother can.”— Sarah Browne, Minister of Amerindian Affairs
  • “put systems in place so this doesn't happen again”— Sarah Browne, Minister of Amerindian Affairs
  • “Given the magnitude of this national tragedy, I am disappointed that neither I, as Leader of the Opposition, nor any parliamentary opposition party was consulted in the establishment of the Commission.”— Leader of the Opposition
  • “The water is not only murky, but muddy”— Mark Phillips, Prime Minister
