Guyanese press news 26 July 2026

Other nations' ministers stepped down following deadly ferry disasters

Kaieteur News

Read the original at Kaieteur News ↗

What this source establishes

A 26 July Kaieteur News compendium of the organised demands for Minister Juan Edghill's resignation, set against international precedent. It records that on 19 July Edghill gave figures of 284 tonnes licensed cargo against 268 tonnes manifested and 397 licensed passengers against 116 aboard, then conceded the next day that the manifest did not reflect the number of people on board. It is the piece that identifies the statutory basis being proposed: the AFC called for a Commission of Inquiry under the Commission of Inquiry Act, Chapter 19:03. It sets out ministerial resignation precedents after ferry disasters in Gabon (2023), South Korea (2014) and Senegal (2002).

Figures cited in this source

73 lives claimed, dozens still missing; Mohamed says toll expected to surpass 100; Sweatnam cites 'over one hundred persons dead'

Key claims

  • On July 19 Minister Juan Edghill said the MV Barima was licensed to carry 284 tonnes of cargo with a manifest showing 268 tonnes on board, and was licensed for 397 passengers with only 116 aboard; the following day he acknowledged the manifest did not accurately reflect the number of people on board.
  • Social activist Sherlina Nageer and a small group of women were arrested while protesting for Edghill's removal during the National Prayer Service at the Kingston Seawall Bandstand, and were subsequently released on self-bail.
  • The joint opposition comprising WIN, FGM, AFC, VPAC and other opposition representatives demanded Edghill's immediate resignation.
  • Opposition Leader Azruddin Mohamed said he believes Edghill should be held criminally responsible: 'Edghill should have been in the lockup since this incident happened.'
  • Dr Terrence Campbell (APNU MP) said 'accountability must go from top to bottom' and 'It cannot be that we are so dependent on the great bishop that no one else can do what he's doing right now', referring to Edghill, a bishop in the Assemblies of God.
  • FGM leader and MP Amanza Walton said she would have tendered her resignation, and that Ali should have immediately required the minister, the head of MARAD and the head of the Transport and Harbours Division to proceed on leave.
  • WIN MP Janelle Sweatnam called for Edghill's immediate resignation along with that of MARAD Director Stephen Thomas.
  • The Alliance For Change called for the immediate removal of Edghill and MARAD Director General Stephen Thomas, and proposed establishing a Commission of Inquiry under the Commission of Inquiry Act, Chapter 19:03.
  • The Amerindian Peoples Association called for Edghill's removal and asked: 'Who oversees the operations of the vessels that they do not detect what appears to be corrupt practices in overloading?'
  • The APA said the normalisation of corrupt practices, which Edghill described as 'mischief', has consistently contributed to the systemic failures behind the tragedy.
  • Comparative precedents cited: Gabon's Transport Minister Brice Paillat resigned in March 2023 a week after the Esther Miracle ferry sank (at least 21 killed, 31 missing); South Korean Prime Minister Chung Hong-won resigned after the Sewol sinking of 16 April 2014 (476 aboard, at least 187 confirmed dead); Senegal's Transport Minister Youssouf Sakho and Armed Forces Minister Youba Sambou resigned after the MS Joola capsized in September 2002 (roughly 970 of an estimated 1,034 aboard dead or missing).

Quotations

  • “Before we reach the parliamentary level, Edghill must go. Edghill must resign. He must be held accountable.”— Azruddin Mohamed, Leader of the Opposition
  • “It cannot be that we are so dependent on the great bishop that no one else can do what he's doing right now.”— Dr Terrence Campbell, APNU MP, referring to Edghill, a bishop in the Assemblies of God
  • “I would have offered the president my resignation. It's really that simple. I think this matter has been handled poorly, from the top down, from the president on down.”— Amanza Walton, Forward Guyana Movement leader and MP
  • “One life lost is one too many. We have over one hundred persons dead, and we are here to speak for them.”— Janelle Sweatnam, WIN MP
  • “Who oversees the operations of the vessels that they do not detect what appears to be corrupt practices in overloading?”— Amerindian Peoples Association, statement
  • “The right thing for me to do is to take responsibility and resign as a person who is in charge of the cabinet.”— Chung Hong-won, then Prime Minister of South Korea, 2014
