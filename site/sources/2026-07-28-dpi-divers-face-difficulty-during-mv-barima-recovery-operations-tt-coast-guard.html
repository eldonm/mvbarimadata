Divers face difficulty during MV Barima recovery operations – T&T Coast Guard

What this source establishes

A state release of 28 July carrying the first first-hand account in this corpus of conditions at the wreck from the diving side: a Trinidad and Tobago Coast Guard officer describing visibility of under a foot, strong tidal currents at the river mouth and shallow water defeating visual search. It matters to the wreck question because these are the conditions later relied on, and it is the state's own account of them. It also carries an asset count of 17 — 14 vessels and three aircraft — against 18 in the Prime Minister's release of the same day, a small unreconciled divergence between two government releases published hours apart.

Key claims

  • Lieutenant (N) **Tyler Ferraz** of the Trinidad and Tobago Coast Guard on conditions at the wreck: "a lot of the divers are unable to see their hands in front of their face, anything [not] more than a foot".
  • The obstacles given are limited visibility from a muddy seabed, strong tidal currents near the river mouth, and shallow water complicating visual search.
  • The deployment is described as 17 primary assets — 14 vessels and three aircraft — with specialised local and international dive teams.
  • [CONTESTED] The asset count of **17** here sits against **18** in the Prime Minister's release of the same day. Both are state releases dated 28 July. Neither reconciles the other.
  • This is the first first-hand account in this corpus from a diver's side of the conditions later cited in support of leaving the wreck in place, and it is dated one day *before* the French team's assessment of 27 July was reported.
