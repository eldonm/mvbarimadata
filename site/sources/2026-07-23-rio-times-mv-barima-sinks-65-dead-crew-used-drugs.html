Guyana Ferry MV Barima Sinks: 65 Dead, Crew Used Drugs

The Rio Times (Brazil) · Ana Clara Santos

Read the original at The Rio Times (Brazil) ↗

What this source establishes

A Brazilian English-language treatment framing the disaster around the commission of inquiry and crew fitness. It notes Brazil and Trinidad and Tobago sent help, giving the Brazilian contribution more prominence than Anglophone wires did. It reports officials framing the inquiry partly as a safeguard against evidence tampering, a rationale absent from most other records in this corpus. It closes by arguing that ferries are a survival matter for communities with no alternatives, and that the inquiry could force an overhaul of ticketing and boarding rules nationwide.

Figures cited in this source

Confirmed death toll 65 as of July 22; around 76 survived; manifest listed 133 but an estimated 179 aboard

Key claims

  • Confirmed death toll climbed to 65 by July 22, with fears it could go higher — Prime Minister Mark Phillips
  • Around 76 of those aboard survived
  • Neighboring Brazil and Trinidad and Tobago sent help as the recovery effort widened
  • The government will establish an independent commission of inquiry as soon as possible to determine what happened before departure and whether operating procedures were followed
  • The aim is to safeguard the integrity of the investigation and prevent any tampering with evidence — officials
  • The captain and at least one other crew member were taken into custody after testing positive for marijuana; all staff involved in managing and loading the vessel were suspended
  • The MV Barima first took to the water in 1939, making it over 85 years old at the time of the disaster
  • The official manifest listed only 133 passengers yet the ferry was carrying an estimated 179 people when it capsized off the Essequibo coast
  • For remote parts of Guyana, ferries are a lifeline and their safety is a matter of survival for communities with few alternatives
