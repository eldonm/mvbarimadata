APA CALLS FOR EDGHILL'S REMOVAL OVER MV BARIMA TRAGEDY

What this source establishes

HGPTV reports the Amerindian Peoples Association demanding that Minister Juan Edghill step down and calling for an independent review of Guyana's river and sea transport. The APA says North-West District passengers had complained for years about vessel condition and crew behaviour, and calls the disaster 'a tragedy waiting to happen'. It argues the sinking exposes transport inequity for Indigenous hinterland residents who cannot afford air alternatives, and insists Indigenous communities be included in reform discussions. The report notes the APA seeks removal on grounds of ministerial responsibility rather than personal legal culpability.

Key claims

  • The APA demands Minister Juan Edghill step down and calls for an independent examination of Guyana's river and sea transportation system.
  • The APA says North-West District passengers had raised concerns for years about vessel condition, crew behaviour and unsafe travel circumstances, and characterises the disaster as 'a tragedy waiting to happen'.
  • The APA says the disaster revealed transportation inequities affecting Indigenous hinterland residents who depend on ferries because air travel and alternatives cost significantly more.
  • APA quoted: 'Safe transportation is not a privilege; it is a fundamental right that every person in Guyana deserves.'
  • The APA identifies the inaccurate passenger manifest as evidence of management failures, noting authorities initially recorded 116 passengers and 17 crew before discovering dozens of unlisted survivors.
  • The APA references potential 'corrupt practices in overloading'; the report notes the government has not established the ferry exceeded capacity, that Edghill denied overloading, and that cargo records were being reviewed.
  • The report states Edghill maintained no technical report declared the vessel unserviceable, with its last dry-dock in 2024 and another planned for later in 2026.
  • The APA requests review of vessel maintenance, safety standards, licensing, crew training, passenger-capacity enforcement, weather monitoring and emergency preparedness across the water-transportation network, and similar examinations of other river services nationwide.
  • The APA insists investigators be independent of the agencies scrutinised, with transparent, swift proceedings and authority to access records.
  • The APA argues Indigenous communities must participate in reform discussions as regular users who understand the practical dangers.
  • The APA calls for sustained investment in modern vessels, wharves, communications and emergency systems, plus stricter inspections and mandatory accessible safety equipment.
  • The report notes the APA presented no evidence Edghill personally caused the capsizing and frames removal as ministerial responsibility rather than legal culpability.
  • APA quoted: 'The loss of lives aboard the MV Barima must become a turning point.'
