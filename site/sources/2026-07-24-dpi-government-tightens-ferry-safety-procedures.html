Official document operational update 24 July 2026

Government tightens ferry safety procedures following MV Barima tragedy

Department of Public Information

Read the original at Department of Public Information ↗

What this source establishes

The state's first concrete regulatory response after the sinking, and its clearest statement of failure at the point of embarkation. It confirms that individuals boarded the MV Barima without being ticketed and that CCTV footage from the departure stelling established 179 people aboard, reviewed overnight with the Prime Minister participating personally. It commits the Commission of Inquiry to investigating how manifest discrepancies were allowed to occur at T&HD. The remedy announced is stricter compliance with existing procedures rather than new rules, while all other ferry routes continue operating.

Official statement recorded in this source

A review of CCTV footage from the departure stelling established that 179 people were on board the vessel when it sank; it was confirmed that individuals boarded the MV Barima without being ticketed.

Figures cited in this source

179 people on board the vessel when it sank (per CCTV review)

Key claims

  • Government has begun strengthening compliance with standard operating procedures across the country's ferry service.
  • It was confirmed that individuals boarded the MV Barima without being ticketed.
  • A review of CCTV footage from the departure stelling established that 179 people were on board the vessel when it sank.
  • The footage was examined overnight by a team of officials, with the Prime Minister also participating in the review.
  • The independent Commission of Inquiry will investigate how discrepancies between the official passenger manifest and the actual number of passengers were allowed to occur at the Transport and Harbours Department (T&HD).
  • Other ferry routes continue to operate while measures are implemented.
