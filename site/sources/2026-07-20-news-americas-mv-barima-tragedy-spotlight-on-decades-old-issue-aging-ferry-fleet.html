What this source establishes

A News Americas Now fleet-condition analysis, the most widely syndicated version of the aging-fleet framing in the Caribbean press and a likely origin of several build years now in circulation. It gives the MV Barima as built in Scotland in 1939 and 87 years old, records the 2017 rehabilitation at G$150.6 million covering hull work, new engines and passenger accommodation, and dates the MV Malali to 1962 and MV Makouria to 1959, both subject to frequent mechanical failures. It notes French military personnel including 12 specialist divers joined the rescue under Lt Cdr Alexis Touboul.

Key claims

  • MV Barima built in Scotland 1939, 87 years old
  • 2017 rehabilitation cost G$150.6 million: hull work, new engines, passenger accommodation improvements
  • MV Malali given as 1962 and MV Makouria as 1959, both subject to frequent mechanical failures
  • MV Ma Lisha (2023) and MV Konawaruk 1899 (2025) have not addressed all routes
  • French military personnel including 12 specialist divers joined the rescue under Lt Cdr Alexis Touboul
  • Minister Edghill said the vessel operated within maintenance schedules and dismissed unseaworthiness claims

Prior warnings recorded

  • Port Kaituma residents began requesting a newer, faster vessel as early as 2010. By 2015, government officials acknowledged the aging fleet required urgent replacement, yet the MV Barima remained operational until the disaster.
