Regional press news 20 July 2026

67 rescued as Guyana launches major search operation after MV Barima capsizes with 133 people aboard

Caribbean National Weekly · Joanne Clark

Read the original at Caribbean National Weekly ↗

What this source establishes

A same-day companion piece from Caribbean National Weekly reproducing the government's pre-scandal safety case in full, including the claim that the vessel was within all approved limits. Read with the outlet's other 20 July story, the pair shows the same outlet carrying the official denial and the criminal-manifest allegation simultaneously. The headline's '133 people aboard' sits against a body that says 116 passengers plus 17 crew. It notes investigations would only begin after search and rescue concluded.

Figures cited in this source

67 rescued including 15 children (41 males, 11 females, 15 children); 116 passengers and 17 crew aboard; no death toll given

Key claims

  • 'From what we hear, it's a big wave. That's all we hear' — Public Works Minister Juan Edghill
  • Preliminary information does not indicate engine failure, mechanical problem or overloading — authorities
  • Vessel had cargo capacity of 284 tonnes with a manifest showing 268 tonnes — Juan Edghill
  • MV Barima is licensed to carry more than 300 passengers; only 116 passengers were aboard with 17 crew — Juan Edghill
  • 'The operation will continue until every effort has been exhausted' — Prime Minister Mark Phillips
  • Search area expanded from 400 square kilometres to 1,070 square kilometres
  • Three command centres established at Mabaruma, Port Kaituma and the Amerindian Hostel at Umana Yana in Georgetown
  • Vessel was equipped with 250 life jackets, six inflatable life rafts and two rigid life rafts; last dry-docked in 2024; operates with new engines making three to four trips monthly — officials

Quotations

  • “From what we hear, it's a big wave. That's all we hear”— Juan Edghill, Public Works Minister
  • “There was no engine problem or any mechanical problem”— Juan Edghill, Public Works Minister
  • “The operation will continue until every effort has been exhausted”— Mark Phillips, Prime Minister
