What this source establishes

A Kaieteur News column arguing that seaworthiness turns on structural condition, maintenance quality, safety compliance, inspections and repairs rather than construction date, and that 'age is not evidence of unseaworthiness; qualified engineers must determine seaworthiness through inspection'. It restates the Barima rehabilitation sequence of G$150m in 2017, engine repairs in 2021, G$90m emergency repairs in 2022, hull work in 2024 and G$124.5m budgeted for 2026, dates the MV Lady Northcote to 1937 and the MV Torani to 1960, and cites Washington State Ferries operating 1950s-era vessels. It is the principal published counter to the 'Built for 1939' framing in this corpus.

Key claims

  • Argues seaworthiness depends on structural condition, maintenance quality, safety compliance, inspections and repairs — not age
  • Barima rehabilitation sequence: G$150m in 2017, engine repairs 2021, G$90m emergency repairs 2022, hull work 2024, G$124.5m budgeted 2026
  • MV Lady Northcote given as built 1937 and MV Torani as commissioned 1960, both said to continue operating after rehabilitation
  • Cites Washington State Ferries operating 1950s-era vessels
  • Urges investigation of maintenance records and physical condition rather than assumptions from construction date
