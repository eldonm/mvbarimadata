Historical & context letter to the editor / vessel repair chronology 23 July 2026

THE MV BARIMA TRAGEDY COULD HAVE BEEN AVOIDED

Kaieteur News · Leyland Chitlall Roopnaraine (letter to the editor)

Read the original at Kaieteur News ↗

What this source establishes

In a letter to Kaieteur News, Leyland Chitlall Roopnaraine sets out a detailed repair chronology for the MV Barima: built by Ferguson Brothers (Port Glasgow) Ltd in 1939 and registered and launched in British Guiana on 17 November 1939, with ownership assumed by the government of Guyana in 1966; a 2017 breakdown repaired at GY$150 million, emergency engine component replacement in 2021, two weeks of emergency repairs at GY$90 million in 2022, critical repairs at Parika in 2024, and dry-docking slated for late 2026 at GY$124.5 million. No other source in this corpus gives the launch and registration date or the 1966 transfer of ownership.

Key claims

  • Built by Ferguson Brothers (Port Glasgow) Ltd in 1939; registered and launched in British Guiana on 17 November 1939
  • In 1966, after Independence, the government of Guyana assumed ownership
  • Broke down 2017, repaired at GY$150 million, returned to commission
  • 2021: major engine components replaced in an emergency
  • 2022: portions seriously deteriorated; two weeks of emergency repairs at GY$90 million
  • 2024: critical repairs in the Parika dockyard
  • Slated for dry-docking late 2026 and upgrade at GY$124.5 million
  • Edghill told Parliament in 2023 that the US$12.7m replacement MV Ma Lisha was en route from Calcutta, and travelled to India to attest to her seaworthiness
  • Alleges a documented history of water intake, passenger and cargo overload, and unrecorded passenger manifests — no document cited

Quotations

  • “How else can anyone explain an 87-year-old boat ferrying some 130 persons on a 12-hour journey which was earmarked for major repairs a few months before it capsized?”
  • “They also believe that alcohol did not erode a hull or allow water to pour into the vessel.”

Prior warnings recorded

  • Asserts (post-disaster) that 'many transportation experts believe that the tragedy could have been avoided' and that the vessel had a documented prior history of water intake and overloading. Does not name those experts or cite the documents. Cites Minister Edghill's 2023 parliamentary statement that the US$12.7m replacement vessel MV Ma Lisha was en route from Calcutta as evidence the replacement commitment was made and not honoured.
