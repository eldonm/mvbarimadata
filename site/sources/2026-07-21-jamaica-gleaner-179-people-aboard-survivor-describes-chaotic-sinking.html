Guyana says capsized ferry had 179 people aboard as survivor describes chaotic sinking

NON-VERBATIM RETRIEVAL: condensed rendering only; see file header. The Jamaica Gleaner's placement of the AP Charity dispatch, evidence of Caribbean regional uptake of the survivor testimony on the same day AP filed it. It preserves the Moonsammy and Atkinson quotes and President Ali's national address. Where AP names Moonsammy as a grandmother who lost four grandchildren, this rendering does not carry that relationship, an example of detail attriting in syndication.

Figures cited in this source

27 bodies recovered including four children; at least 69 rescued; approximately 179 aboard; manifest showed 116 passengers and 17 crew

Key claims

  • 'I was fighting for my life. The boat sunk in minutes, seconds' — Helena Moonsammy, survivor, who said boxes fell on her during the chaos
  • 27 bodies recovered including four children — Prime Minister Mark Phillips
  • At least 69 rescued after the vessel sank Saturday off the Essequibo coast
  • Authorities estimate approximately 179 people were aboard the 87-year-old ferry though the manifest showed 116 passengers and 17 crew
  • 'Brace yourself for the coming days. It is going to be a difficult period for us as a people and as a country' — President Irfaan Ali, addressing the nation
  • Alicia Atkinson, 18, reported her three-year-old daughter went missing but she saved her 10-month-old son while floating on debris for approximately eight hours
  • The rescued captain and at least one crew member tested positive for marijuana and remain in police custody
  • All personnel involved in ferry management and loading suspended following suspicion of unauthorized passenger and cargo placement — Public Works Minister Juan Edghill
  • Passengers have long complained about unofficial ticket sales without manifest documentation at state-run ferry terminals
  • The MV Barima underwent dry dock overhaul in 2024 and faced scheduled major repairs for October
