Death toll in Guyana ferry tragedy climbs as regional recovery effort expands

The Nation (Barbados) / nationnews.com · Nationnews Desk

Read the original at The Nation (Barbados) / nationnews.com ↗

What this source establishes

NON-VERBATIM RETRIEVAL: condensed rendering only; see file header. The clearest single account in this corpus of the recovery operation's regional scale, putting 19 vessels and three aircraft on station with Trinidad and Tobago Coast Guard and Brazilian Navy assets. No other source in this corpus gives the identification pipeline in this detail: 44 victims positively identified and 23 released to families for burial. It documents the three days of national mourning and the salvage-airbag plan to restore buoyancy before towing.

Figures cited in this source

Death toll 65 after three more bodies recovered Wednesday; 76 survivors; 44 victims positively identified and 23 released to families

Key claims

  • Maritime assets from Trinidad and Tobago's Coast Guard and the Brazilian Navy are assisting; the effort involves 19 vessels and three aircraft
  • Death toll reached 65 with three additional bodies recovered Wednesday; 76 survivors; 44 victims positively identified and 23 released to families for burial — authorities
  • Recovery plan involves repositioning the wreck and attaching salvage airbags to restore buoyancy before towing it closer to shore
  • 'One of the darkest moments in Guyana's recent history' — Prime Minister Mark Phillips, at a National Interfaith Prayer Service at Kingston Seawall Bandstand
  • President Mohamed Irfaan Ali declared three days of national mourning with flags at half-mast
  • The government committed to deploying 'every available resource' until recovery operations conclude
  • Additional memorial services scheduled for Port Kaituma on Thursday and Mabaruma on Friday

Quotations

  • “one of the darkest moments in Guyana's recent history”— Mark Phillips, Prime Minister, at the National Interfaith Prayer Service, Kingston Seawall Bandstand
  • “every available resource”— the government, on its commitment until recovery operations conclude
