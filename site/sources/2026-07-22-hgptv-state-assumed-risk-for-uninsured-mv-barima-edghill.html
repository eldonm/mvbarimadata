Guyanese press news 22 July 2026

STATE ASSUMED RISK FOR UNINSURED MV BARIMA — EDGHILL

HGPTV Nightly News · HGPTV

Read the original at HGPTV Nightly News ↗

What this source establishes

HGP TV reports Public Works Minister Juan Edghill confirming on Tuesday that the state-owned MV Barima carried no commercial insurance and that the Government of Guyana therefore carried the financial risk itself. Edghill said it has long been the practice for state-owned vessels to operate without commercial insurance, with government assuming losses and any compensation. The report notes officials did not say whether a dedicated self-insurance mechanism exists, or what compensation is available to survivors and families. At the same briefing Prime Minister Mark Phillips called the manifest failure 'a breakdown in the system', with 53 bodies recovered and 76 survivors verified.

Key claims

  • The Government of Guyana carried the financial risk for the MV Barima because the state-owned ferry was not insured when it capsized, Public Works Minister Juan Edghill confirmed on Tuesday.
  • Edghill said it has long been the practice for state-owned vessels to operate without commercial insurance, and under that arrangement the government assumes responsibility for losses and any compensation arising from an incident.
  • The cost of replacing the MV Barima, covering lost government cargo and meeting any compensation obligations could fall directly on the state and ultimately taxpayers.
  • Officials did not say whether the government maintains a dedicated fund or formal self-insurance mechanism for maritime accidents.
  • It remains unclear what compensation may be available to survivors and the families of those who died.
  • Numerous rescued passengers were not listed on the official manifest, making it difficult to establish how many people were actually aboard or how many remained missing during the critical early stages of the search.
  • Phillips described the discrepancy as 'a breakdown in the system' and said it would be examined by the Commission of Inquiry.
  • The inquiry is expected to determine how passengers boarded without being properly recorded, identify those responsible and recommend disciplinary action and changes to existing procedures.
  • Passengers purchasing tickets in person were not required to present identification.
  • The Transport and Harbours Department had begun testing equipment capable of scanning national identification and driver's licence cards, but the system had not been fully introduced before the tragedy.
  • As of 6 p.m. Tuesday, the government said 53 bodies had been recovered and 76 survivors verified.
  • Additional support from Trinidad and Tobago and Brazil was expected to reinforce the teams already deployed.

Quotations

  • “a breakdown in the system”— Prime Minister Brigadier (Ret'd) Mark Phillips, on rescued passengers missing from the official manifest

Actors named
