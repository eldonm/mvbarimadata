Guyanese press letter 25 July 2026

MV Barima tragedy underscores urgent need for stricter ferry safety standards

Kaieteur News · Daniel Singh (letter writer; no further description given in the piece)

Read the original at Kaieteur News ↗

What this source establishes

In a short letter to Kaieteur News, Daniel Singh treats the sinking as an instance of a global pattern of preventable domestic ferry deaths and sets out an enforcement checklist rather than assigning political blame. He calls for vessel lifespan limits, attention to weight distribution and visibility, restrictions on late-night travel, and crew training in maintenance and emergency response, and describes life jackets for all passengers as 'non-negotiable'. It is one of the few letters in this corpus to name night sailing as a risk factor.

The author’s argument, as stated

Preventable domestic ferry deaths require strict enforcement of specific maritime safety standards — vessel lifespan limits, weight distribution, visibility, restrictions on late-night travel, crew training and life jackets for all passengers.

Key claims

  • Says hundreds of lives are lost annually in preventable domestic ferry accidents.
  • Argues infrastructure modernisation must prioritise safety to protect lives and maintain public trust.
  • Lists standards to be strictly enforced: limiting vessel lifespans, respecting weight distributions, ensuring proper visibility, restricting late-night travel, and training crew in maintenance and emergency response.
  • Calls life jackets for all passengers 'non-negotiable'.
  • Frames safety as a collaborative, international effort.

Quotations

  • “basic standards like providing life jackets for all passengers are non-negotiable”— Daniel Singh, letter writer
  • “A ferry service should benefit the public, not cause harm.”— Daniel Singh, letter writer
