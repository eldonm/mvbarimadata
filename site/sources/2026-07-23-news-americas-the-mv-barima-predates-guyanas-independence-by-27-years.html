What this source establishes

A News Americas Now historical context piece giving the fullest press account in this corpus of the vessel's colonial provenance: constructed in 1939 at the Ferguson Brothers shipyard, Port Glasgow, 27 years before independence on 26 May 1966, as part of a three-ship order placed via the Crown Agents for the Colonies for British Guiana Railways' Transport and Harbors Department, with sister ships Pomeroon and Lady Northcote, twin-screw and cruising at about 8.5 knots. Its details match the Paxman engine register almost item for item. It compares the disaster to the MV Christena sinking of 1 August 1970 between St Kitts and Nevis, which killed 233.

Key claims

  • Constructed 1939 at Ferguson Brothers shipyard, Port Glasgow, Scotland — 27 years before independence on 26 May 1966
  • Commissioned as part of a three-ship order via the Crown Agents for the Colonies for British Guiana Railways' Transport and Harbors Department
  • Sister ships were the Pomeroon and Lady Northcote
  • Twin-screw, cruising speed about 8.5 knots, built for modest colonial-era loads
  • Carried 179 people and cargo when she capsized
  • Compares the disaster to the MV Christena sinking of 1 August 1970 between St Kitts and Nevis, which killed 233
  • PM Mark Phillips confirmed investigation into potential criminal negligence and institutional failures in the maritime administration
