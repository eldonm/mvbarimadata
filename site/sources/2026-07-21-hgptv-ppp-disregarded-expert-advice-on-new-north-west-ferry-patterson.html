What this source establishes

An HGPTV interview in which former Public Infrastructure Minister David Patterson alleges that two technical evaluations recommended a costlier, better-designed ferry than the MV Ma Lisha, and that procurement reverted to the cheaper vessel after the PPP/C returned to office in August 2020: 'Immediately on the PPP's return, they reverted to the cheaper version.' He gives the two bids under Indian financing as about US$15.3 million and close to US$12 million, and says the coalition's plan to semi-retire the Barima to the Berbice River was never implemented. The underlying evaluations are unpublished. The report notes Edghill maintained no report declared the ferry unseaworthy.

Key claims

  • Two bids received under Indian financing: about US$15.3 million and close to US$12 million
  • Technical experts recommended the higher-priced proposal for superior design and capability; a second independent assessment after challenge reached the same conclusion
  • Patterson says procurement reverted to the cheaper vessel after the PPP/C returned to office in August 2020
  • MV Ma Lisha built by India's Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers Limited for about US$12.7 million, commissioned 2023
  • The coalition plan was to semi-retire the Barima to the Berbice River for one or two years; never implemented
  • Edghill maintained no report declared the ferry unseaworthy and it was within its maintenance cycle

Quotations

  • “Immediately on the PPP's return, they reverted to the cheaper version.”
  • “the more expensive vessel”

Prior warnings recorded

  • David Patterson, former Minister of Public Infrastructure, in an interview with HGPTV Nightly News published 21 July 2026 (i.e. AFTER the disaster, but describing pre-disaster technical advice): two separate technical evaluations under the APNU+AFC administration recommended the more expensive of two Indian-financed ferry bids on grounds of superior design and capability, and a second independent assessment reached the same conclusion after the lower bidder challenged the decision. Patterson says procurement reverted to the cheaper vessel after the PPP/C returned to office in August 2020. NOTE: this is a post-disaster allegation about pre-disaster advice; the underlying technical evaluations have not been published and this archive has not seen them.
