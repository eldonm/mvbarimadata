Regional press wire 20 July 2026

27 dead, 83 missing from capsized Guyana ferry, says PM

Jamaica Observer · AFP

Read the original at Jamaica Observer ↗

What this source establishes

An AFP dispatch in which Prime Minister Phillips raises the toll from 10 to 27 and states for the first time that he is confident 179 people were aboard, against a manifest of 133. It records the arrival of French military divers from French Guiana and the near-tripling of the search area. It preserves Edghill's timeline detail that the distress call came about eight hours after a mid-afternoon departure. The 'previous 10' figure it supersedes is not otherwise well attested in this corpus.

Figures cited in this source

At least 27 dead (raised from a previous 10); 69 rescued; 83 missing; 179 aboard; manifest listed 133

Key claims

  • At least 27 people died and 83 remain missing; 69 rescued — Prime Minister Mark Phillips, Monday
  • 'Our focus again is on finding people... We still have hope' — Mark Phillips, having raised the death toll from a previous 10
  • Phillips said he was now 'confident' that 179 people were aboard
  • The ship's passenger manifest listing 133 people on board was inaccurate — Public Works Minister Juan Edghill
  • Search area expanded from 400 to 1,070 square kilometers (154 to 413 square miles); local fishermen joined recovery efforts
  • The French army sent 12 divers, two doctors and a mechanic from nearby French Guiana — the presidency
  • Two crewmembers tested positive for marijuana after the incident — Mark Phillips, Sunday
  • The ship began its journey in mid-afternoon and a distress call was received about eight hours later — Juan Edghill
  • The ferry was built in 1939, according to the shipping website VesselFinder

About this archive

An independent, citation-first record of the sinking of the MV Barima on the night of Saturday 18 July 2026, compiled from 209 published documents and checked line by line against them. It is not affiliated with the Government of Guyana, the Transport & Harbours Department, any political party, or any news organisation.

Compiled Sunday 26 July 2026, last revised Tuesday 28 July 2026. The Commission of Inquiry’s members were named on 26 July 2026, its establishing instrument was not yet gazetted, and figures were still moving. Read Method & gaps before citing anything here.
