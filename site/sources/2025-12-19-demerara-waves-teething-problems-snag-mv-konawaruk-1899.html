What this source establishes

A Demerara Waves report seven months before the disaster on the MV Konawaruk 1899, a second-hand Greek-built roll-on/roll-off ferry bought for just under US$5 million for the Parika-Supenaam route. On its inaugural crossing the bow door failed to lower properly at both terminals, with greenheart planks used at Parika and more planks at Supenaam and delays of over 15 minutes; the MARAD Director-General inspected the mooring discrepancy. The article records that a second Greek ferry was expected the following month for the Georgetown-North West service. This is the shore-infrastructure problem later cited in accounts of why the Kalliopi N remained uncommissioned.

Key claims

  • MV Konawaruk 1899 (ex-ARIS IV) Greek-built, purchased for just under US$5 million, for Parika-Supenaam
  • Named for Konawaruk in Region Eight; '1899' references the arbitral award on the Guyana-Venezuela boundary
  • On the inaugural crossing the bow door failed to lower properly at BOTH terminals; greenheart planks used at Parika, more planks at Supenaam; delays over 15 minutes
  • MARAD Director-General inspected the mooring discrepancy
  • MV Sabanto and MV Kanawan, about 20 years old, would continue on the same route

Quotations

  • “the door did not land flat on the bridge”

Prior warnings recorded

  • Not a warning about the Barima, but a documented pre-disaster instance of the fleet-renewal programme delivering vessels that did not mate correctly with existing shore infrastructure — the same wharf-compatibility problem later cited as a reason the Kalliopi N sat uncommissioned while the Barima kept sailing.
