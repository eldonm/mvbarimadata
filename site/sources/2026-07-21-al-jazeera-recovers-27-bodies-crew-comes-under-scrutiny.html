International press news 21 July 2026

Guyana recovers 27 bodies after ferry disaster as crew comes under scrutiny

Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera Staff, AP and Reuters

Read the original at Al Jazeera ↗

What this source establishes

Al Jazeera's desk write-through of AP and Reuters copy, putting the manifest gap at 32 unrecorded survivors and giving 69 rescued against 83 missing. It is one of the clearest statements in this corpus that passengers had complained for years about cash seat sales at state terminals. It includes President Ali's hospital visit and his description of the encounter as deeply emotional, and preserves a photo caption crediting AFP.

Figures cited in this source

27 bodies recovered; 179 aboard; 69 rescued; 83 missing

Key claims

  • Authorities recovered 27 bodies from a ferry that capsized with 179 people on board — Guyanese authorities
  • 69 people rescued and 83 still missing as of Monday — officials
  • Sinking is the country's worst marine disaster in decades — Minister of Public Works Juan Edghill
  • 32 people rescued from the disaster were not recorded in the ferry's manifest, which had listed 116 passengers and 17 crew — officials
  • The ferry's captain and at least one other crew member were taken into custody after testing positive for marijuana — police
  • Passengers had complained for years that officials at state-run ferry terminals were selling seats for cash without recording true passenger numbers, which Edghill called 'a criminal action'
  • Prime Minister Mark Phillips launched an investigation into potential negligence by the crew
  • President Irfaan Ali visited survivors and families in hospital and called it 'a deeply emotional experience'

Quotations

  • “We have a zero tolerance for members of the crew drinking or smoking while at work. And for us to have positive tests from the captain, this is serious”— Juan Edghill, Public Works Minister
  • “a criminal action”— Juan Edghill, Public Works Minister, on unrecorded seat sales
  • “It was a deeply emotional experience. I listened to their stories, shared in their pain and anxiety”— Irfaan Ali, President
