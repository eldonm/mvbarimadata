What this source establishes

An opinion column published under Kaieteur News's “Peeping Tom” byline, carrying the paper's disclaimer, arguing against the Minister's resignation at this stage. It distinguishes collective from individual ministerial responsibility, argues the Transport & Harbours Department's own board insulates the Minister from day-to-day operations, and proposes Cabinet support, National Assembly acceptance and sustained non-partisan public loss of trust as tests. It states the cause is not yet established and cites the 2017 Camp Street prison fire, in which 18 died without a ministerial resignation.

Key claims

  • The only sustained argument AGAINST the Minister's resignation in the corpus, against numerous published demands for it
  • Argues the Transport & Harbours Department's own board insulates the Minister from operational responsibility
  • Proposes a test for political accountability: loss of Cabinet support, National Assembly rejection of explanations, and sustained non-partisan public loss of trust
  • Cites the 2017 Camp Street prison fire, in which 18 died without a ministerial resignation, as the consistency benchmark
  • States a minister cannot be forced to resign by a no-confidence vote in the minister personally, only the government by a vote against the government
  • Notes APNU+AFC did not resign after the 2018 no-confidence motion passed
