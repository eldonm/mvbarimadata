The first substantial survivor-testimony piece in the domestic press, published the same day as the sinking, and the earliest source for the 67-rescued figure including 15 children. It establishes the causal account survivors themselves gave from hour one - a wave struck, the vessel leaned sharply to one side, water rushed in within seconds - and that most passengers were asleep in hammocks. Uniquely names Elena Moonsammy as having lost all seven children she was travelling with, and Orman Cox as having improvised a flotation device from plastic bottles and his belt that five survivors clung to for hours. Cox's account that survivors could see rescue-boat lights but were invisible to rescuers because of the swells, and shouted unheard for hours, is the single most damaging early eyewitness detail about rescue effectiveness.
